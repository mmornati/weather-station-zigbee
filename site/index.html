Why build it

A real weather station, without the cloud.

Commercial stations push your garden's weather through an app and somebody else's server, and often on a WiFi link that drops at the bottom of the garden. This one talks Zigbee to the house and nothing else.

Solar, no cables

One 18650 and a small 6 V panel. About four months on the battery alone, so it goes where the readings are right: in the open.

Zigbee, not WiFi

Joins the mesh your other sensors already use. No passwords, no IP, no account, and it sleeps at 15 µA between reports.

The full set

Wind speed, gust and direction, rain, temperature, humidity and pressure. Optional modules are found at boot: solder only what you want.

Printable

Radiation shield, sealed box, panel bracket, pole saddles and rain gauge plate, in OpenSCAD and sized for a 220 × 220 mm bed.

Everything a forecast needs, from your own garden.

How it works

Asleep 94 % of the time.

The ESP32-C6 sleeps in deep sleep, wakes for each rain tip, samples the wind for 3 s every minute, and sends everything over Zigbee every 5 minutes. That is how one 18650 lasts months without sun.

Step by step

Build it in nine steps.

Each step links to the file you need and to the detailed guide. Bench-test before you print or drill anything.

  1. Order the parts

    About €170–200 including the pole and the filament. The wind and rain kit must be the reed-switch type with RJ11 plugs, not a set with a wireless console. Order the electronics from one shop so you can bench-test while the kit ships.

    Bill of materialsFull parts list

  2. Check the kit cables

    Batches differ. With a multimeter, find the anemometer pair (one beep per turn), the vane pair (688 Ω to 120 kΩ as it turns) and the rain pair (a beep per tip).

    Two checks before soldering

  3. Bench test on a breadboard

    Flash the XIAO, wire the kit, the SHT45 and the BME280 on a breadboard, press RESET and watch the serial monitor: modules found, Zigbee joined, a first report. Spin the cups and tip the bucket.

    Wiring diagramweather_station.inoplatformio.ini

  4. Print the parts

    Twelve part types, about 600 g of PETG in a light colour (not PLA: it softens in summer sun). Print one saddle and the box lid first to check your pole and your inserts.

    Parts & print settingsAll STL files (zip)OpenSCAD sources

  5. Solder the board, build the box

    XIAO, resistors, capacitors and the two RJ11 jacks on a 70 × 50 mm perfboard. Charger, cell holder and BME280 next to it, the NTC taped to the cell. Four PG7 glands and the vent plug underneath, silicone cord in the lid groove.

    Board & box guidePin table

  6. Assemble the radiation shield

    Three M5 rods through the base, then spacer, plate, spacer, plate… nine times, and the cap. The SHT45 sits in the perforated cage in the middle of the stack, out of the sun and the rain.

    Cut-away viewradiation_shield.scad

  7. Pair with Zigbee2MQTT

    Copy the converter to Z2M's external_converters/, restart Z2M, open Permit join and press RESET. The station stays awake 2 minutes for the interview. Name it weather_station.

    weather_station.mjsZ2M guide

  8. Install it in the garden

    A 3 m pole in the open, at least 3 m from walls. Wind arm at the top, shield at 1.5 m on the north side, panel facing south, box glands down, rain gauge level on its own post.

    Heights & rendersSiting guide

  9. Add the Home Assistant package

    Copy the package to /config/packages/, restart and set your altitude. You get rain today/month/year, rain rate, dew point, feels-like, Beaufort, and alerts for gusts, lightning, frost, low battery and a silent station.

    weather_station.yamlEntities & alerts

Charger first. Connect the bq24074 to the cell and the panel and check the cell charges in the sun before you connect the XIAO. The charger refuses to charge below 0 °C on purpose: Li-ion must never charge when frozen.

Flash

No toolchain. Just a USB cable.

Plug the XIAO ESP32-C6 into your computer and flash the latest firmware straight from this page. Prefer to build it yourself? It's a single pio run.

Install the firmware

Works in Chrome and Edge on desktop (Web Serial). On the first install, choose Erase device so the Zigbee stack starts clean. Later updates keep the rain total if you don't erase.

Your browser doesn't support Web Serial. Open this page in Chrome or Edge on a computer. Web Serial needs a secure (https) page.

FAQ

Good questions.

Why Zigbee and not WiFi / ESPHome?

A WiFi report costs several times the energy of a Zigbee one, and garden WiFi is often the weakest link. As a sleepy Zigbee end device the station reports through the nearest router, needs no credentials and lives on a small cell and a palm-sized panel. The trade-off is firmware updates over USB and a small Z2M converter.

Will it survive the winter?

The 18650 alone covers about four months with no sun at all, and a 1 W panel already covers December in northern France. The charger stops charging below 0 °C to protect the cell; the cell carries the station through cold spells and charges again once it warms up.

How accurate is it?

The SHT45 is rated ±0.1 °C and ±1 %RH; in practice the radiation shield and its siting matter more than the sensor. Wind is sampled 3 s every minute, which gives a good 5-minute picture but can miss a short gust between two windows. The rain gauge's 0.28 mm per tip is nominal: compare with a manual gauge and adjust it if needed.

Do I need all the optional modules?

No. The firmware detects what is connected at power-on and only exposes those readings. You can add a module later: press RESET, then run Interview and Reconfigure in Zigbee2MQTT during the 2 awake minutes.

Which printer do I need?

Any printer with a 220 × 220 mm bed that prints PETG. The parts were designed on an Ender 3 V2 Neo with its stock PTFE-lined hotend. ASA works too if your hotend handles about 250 °C. Avoid PLA outdoors.

Is it tested?

Every commit runs host unit tests (vane decoding, wind average, gust and direction, rain bounce guard, the clock across deep sleep), builds the firmware with PlatformIO and Arduino CLI, tests the converter against zigbee-herdsman-converters, validates the Home Assistant package and renders every 3D part.
